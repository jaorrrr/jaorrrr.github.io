2024 Junior Division: Round 1

The Online Knowledge Challenge from the 2024 edition, for students aged 12–14. 90 minutes, 50 marks.

Before you start

  • 90 minutes
  • 50 marks
  • Part A: 10 multiple-choice questions (20 marks)
  • Part B: 4 structured problems (30 marks)

Set a timer and work as a team. You may use a scientific calculator and the data sheet. For Part A, choose an answer and check it. For Part B, write your working on paper, then compare it with the worked solution and mark scheme.

Data sheet

  • 1 kWh = 1000 Wh = 3.6 MJ; 1 tonne (t) = 1000 kg
  • 1 litre of water has a mass of 1 kg
  • Heating 1 kg of water by 1 °C needs 4.18 kJ
  • 1 hectare (ha) = 10 000 m2; 1 km2 = 100 ha

All past papers

Part A: Multiple choice 20 marks

Each question is worth 2 marks. Choose one answer.

Question A1 2 marks

A 2000 W kettle is switched on for 3 minutes. How much energy does it use?

Answer options for question A1

Worked solution for question A1

Answer: B. 3 minutes = 0.05 h. Energy = 2.0 kW × 0.05 h = 0.1 kWh (360 000 J).

Question A2 2 marks

In which layer of the atmosphere is most of the ozone layer found?

Answer options for question A2

Worked solution for question A2

Answer: B. About 90% of atmospheric ozone is in the stratosphere, roughly 15–35 km up, where it absorbs harmful UV radiation.

Question A3 2 marks

Which of these crops does NOT depend on insects for pollination?

Answer options for question A3

Worked solution for question A3

Answer: C. Wheat, like other grasses, is wind-pollinated (and mostly self-pollinating). Apples, almonds and strawberries need insects such as bees.

Question A4 2 marks

A plant has plenty of water in its soil. What happens to its rate of transpiration on a hot, dry, windy day?

Answer options for question A4

Worked solution for question A4

Answer: B. Heat, dry air and wind all steepen the water-vapour gradient between the leaf and the air, so transpiration increases. Stomata close only when the plant is short of water.

Question A5 2 marks

Last year a town produced 600 t of waste and recycled 30% of it. This year the waste fell by 10% and the recycling rate rose to 40%. How much more waste was recycled this year?

Answer options for question A5

Worked solution for question A5

Answer: B. Last year: 0.30 × 600 = 180 t. This year: waste = 540 t, recycled = 0.40 × 540 = 216 t. Difference = 36 t.

Question A6 2 marks

Making one cotton T-shirt uses about 2700 L of water. A class of 30 students each buys 2 fewer T-shirts this year. How much water is saved?

Answer options for question A6

Worked solution for question A6

Answer: C. 30 × 2 × 2700 = 162 000 L, about the volume of a small swimming pool.

Question A7 2 marks

What causes coral bleaching?

Answer options for question A7

Worked solution for question A7

Answer: A. Corals depend on algae called zooxanthellae for food and colour. Heat stress makes the corals expel the algae, leaving the white skeleton visible. The coral may starve if it lasts too long.

Question A8 2 marks

Which item takes the longest to break down in the sea?

Answer options for question A8

Worked solution for question A8

Answer: D. Estimates: paper, weeks; aluminium can, about 200 years; plastic bottle, about 450 years; glass, up to a million years. Glass is inert, so it is far less harmful than plastic.

Question A9 2 marks

On a 1:50 000 map, a forest is a rectangle 3 cm by 2 cm. What is its real area?

Answer options for question A9

Worked solution for question A9

Answer: C. 3 cm = 1.5 km and 2 cm = 1.0 km. Area = 1.5 km2 = 150 ha. Do not scale the area by 50 000; the area scale is 50 0002.

Question A10 2 marks

Which shows the energy transfers in a hydroelectric power station?

Answer options for question A10

Worked solution for question A10

Answer: A. Water stored high up has gravitational potential energy, which becomes kinetic energy as it falls, turning the turbine and generator to produce electrical energy.

Part B: Structured problems 30 marks

Show all your working. Method marks are awarded even if the final answer is wrong.

Question B1: A shorter shower 8 marks

Leo takes an 8-minute shower every day with a shower head that uses 12 L per minute. He switches to a 5-minute shower with an eco shower head that uses 8 L per minute. The water is heated from 15 °C to 40 °C.

  1. (a)

    Part a: How much water does Leo save each day? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B1 part a

    Before: 8 × 12 = 96 L. After: 5 × 8 = 40 L. Saving = 56 L.

    Marking: 1 mark for both volumes; 1 mark for the saving.

  2. (b)

    Part b: How much water does he save in a year? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B1 part b

    56 × 365 = 20 440 L.

    Marking: Allow error carried forward.

  3. (c)

    Part c: Calculate the energy saved in a year by not heating this water, in kWh. 4 marks

    Worked solution for question B1 part c

    Mass = 20 440 kg. Energy = 20 440 × 4.18 × 25 = 2 135 980 kJ. Dividing by 3600 kJ per kWh gives 593 kWh.

    Marking: 1 mark for the mass; 1 mark for ΔT = 25 °C; 1 mark for kJ; 1 mark for converting to kWh.

Question B2: Can trees cancel our emissions? 7 marks

A growing tree absorbs on average 22 kg of CO2 per year. A family's car and heating emit 7.5 t of CO2 per year. A forest has about 400 trees per hectare.

  1. (a)

    Part a: How many trees are needed to absorb the family's emissions? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B2 part a

    7500 kg / 22 kg = 340.9, so 341 trees (always round up).

    Marking: 1 mark for converting tonnes to kg; 1 mark for the answer.

  2. (b)

    Part b: What area of forest is this, in hectares? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B2 part b

    341 / 400 = 0.85 ha (8500 m2), larger than a football pitch.

    Marking: Allow error carried forward.

  3. (c)

    Part c: Give two reasons why planting trees alone is not a solution to climate change. 3 marks

    Worked solution for question B2 part c

    Any two well-explained points: young trees absorb very little for many years; the carbon is released again if the trees burn, die or are cut down; there is not enough land to offset everyone's emissions; cutting emissions is faster and permanent.

    Marking: Up to 2 marks per reason (maximum 3).

Question B3: Counting daisies 7 marks

A field measures 50 m by 40 m. Students place ten 1 m2 quadrats at random and count the daisies in each: 4, 7, 3, 0, 6, 5, 8, 2, 5, 10.

  1. (a)

    Part a: Calculate the area of the field. 1 mark

    Worked solution for question B3 part a

    50 × 40 = 2000 m2.

    Marking: 1 mark.

  2. (b)

    Part b: Calculate the mean number of daisies per m2. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B3 part b

    Total = 50 daisies in 10 m2, so the mean is 5.0 per m2.

    Marking: 1 mark for the total; 1 mark for the mean.

  3. (c)

    Part c: Estimate the total number of daisies in the field. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B3 part c

    5.0 × 2000 = 10 000 daisies.

    Marking: Allow error carried forward.

  4. (d)

    Part d: Why should the quadrats be placed at random, and why is it better to use more quadrats? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B3 part d

    Random placement avoids bias, such as choosing spots with lots of daisies. More quadrats give a more reliable mean because daisies are not evenly spread.

    Marking: 1 mark each.

Question B4: Electric or petrol? 8 marks

An electric car uses 16 kWh per 100 km. Electricity costs €0.22 per kWh and causes 0.25 kg CO2 per kWh. A petrol car uses 6.0 L per 100 km. Petrol costs €1.80 per litre, and burning 1 L releases 2.3 kg CO2.

  1. (a)

    Part a: Calculate the fuel cost per 100 km for each car. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B4 part a

    Electric: 16 × 0.22 = €3.52. Petrol: 6.0 × 1.80 = €10.80.

    Marking: 1 mark each.

  2. (b)

    Part b: Calculate the CO2 per 100 km for each car. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B4 part b

    Electric: 16 × 0.25 = 4.0 kg. Petrol: 6.0 × 2.3 = 13.8 kg.

    Marking: 1 mark each.

  3. (c)

    Part c: A family drives 12 000 km a year. How much money would they save on fuel with the electric car? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B4 part c

    12 000 km = 120 × 100 km. Saving = 120 × (10.80 − 3.52) = 120 × 7.28 = €873.60.

    Marking: 1 mark for the number of 100 km units or the difference per 100 km; 1 mark for the answer.

  4. (d)

    Part d: Give two reasons why the electric car's real climate impact is higher than your answer to (b) suggests. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B4 part d

    Any two of: making the battery causes large emissions; some energy is lost while charging; the grid's emissions vary with time and place; manufacturing the car itself.

    Marking: 1 mark each.
