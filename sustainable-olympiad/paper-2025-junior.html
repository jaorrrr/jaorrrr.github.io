2025 Junior Division: Round 1

The Online Knowledge Challenge from the 2025 edition, for students aged 12–14. 90 minutes, 50 marks.

Before you start

  • 90 minutes
  • 50 marks
  • Part A: 10 multiple-choice questions (20 marks)
  • Part B: 4 structured problems (30 marks)

Set a timer and work as a team. You may use a scientific calculator and the data sheet. For Part A, choose an answer and check it. For Part B, write your working on paper, then compare it with the worked solution and mark scheme.

Data sheet

  • 1 kWh = 1000 Wh = 3.6 MJ; 1 tonne (t) = 1000 kg
  • 1 litre of water has a mass of 1 kg
  • Heating 1 kg of water by 1 °C needs 4.18 kJ
  • 1 hectare (ha) = 10 000 m2; 1 km2 = 100 ha
  • Electricity: 0.25 kg CO2 per kWh (where needed)

Part A: Multiple choice 20 marks

Each question is worth 2 marks. Choose one answer.

Question A1 2 marks

A classroom replaces a 60 W filament bulb with an 8 W LED bulb that gives the same light. The light is on for 5 hours a day, 365 days a year. How much energy is saved in a year?

Answer options for question A1

Worked solution for question A1

Answer: B. Power saved = 60 − 8 = 52 W. Energy = 52 W × 5 h × 365 = 94 900 Wh = 94.9 kWh. Option C is the total energy used by the old bulb, not the saving.

Question A2 2 marks

Which energy source does NOT originally come from sunlight?

Answer options for question A2

Worked solution for question A2

Answer: D. The Sun heats the air unevenly (wind), evaporates water that later flows downhill (hydro) and powers photosynthesis (wood). Tides are caused mainly by the Moon's gravity acting on a rotating Earth.

Question A3 2 marks

In photosynthesis, 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2. How many molecules of oxygen are released for each molecule of glucose made?

Answer options for question A3

Worked solution for question A3

Answer: C. The balanced equation shows 6 O2 molecules for every one glucose molecule.

Question A4 2 marks

In the food chain grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk, the grass stores 100 000 kJ of energy. If 10% of the energy passes from each level to the next, how much reaches the hawk?

Answer options for question A4

Worked solution for question A4

Answer: C. 100 000 → 10 000 (grasshopper) → 1000 (frog) → 100 (snake) → 10 kJ (hawk). There are four transfers, not five.

Question A5 2 marks

Making a can from recycled aluminium instead of from bauxite ore saves about how much of the energy?

Answer options for question A5

Worked solution for question A5

Answer: D. Extracting aluminium from its ore needs huge amounts of electricity for electrolysis. Melting down scrap needs only about 5% of that, saving about 95%.

Question A6 2 marks

Which greenhouse gas is the most abundant in Earth's atmosphere?

Answer options for question A6

Worked solution for question A6

Answer: C. Water vapour is the most abundant greenhouse gas. Its amount is controlled by temperature, so it amplifies warming caused by CO2 rather than starting it.

Question A7 2 marks

Seawater has a density of about 1.025 g cm−3. Which plastic will float in the sea?

Answer options for question A7

Worked solution for question A7

Answer: C. Only materials less dense than seawater float: polypropylene (0.90 < 1.025). This is why bottle caps are often found on beaches while the bottles sink, unless air is trapped inside.

Question A8 2 marks

A flight emits 0.15 kg CO2 per passenger per km. Lisbon to Paris is 1450 km. How much CO2 is emitted for one passenger making a return trip?

Answer options for question A8

Worked solution for question A8

Answer: C. Return distance = 2 × 1450 = 2900 km. 2900 × 0.15 = 435 kg. Option B is a one-way trip.

Question A9 2 marks

Two meadows each contain 100 plants of the same 4 species. Meadow X has 25 of each species. Meadow Y has 97 of one species and 1 of each of the others. Which statement is correct?

Answer options for question A9

Worked solution for question A9

Answer: B. Biodiversity depends on richness (number of species) and evenness (how equally they are represented). Richness is equal, but X is far more even, so it is more diverse.

Question A10 2 marks

Why do tropical rainforest soils often become poor for farming only a few years after the forest is cleared?

Answer options for question A10

Worked solution for question A10

Answer: B. In rainforests, nutrients are recycled quickly and held mainly in the vegetation. Once the trees are removed, heavy rainfall leaches the few remaining nutrients from the thin topsoil.

Part B: Structured problems 30 marks

Show all your working. Method marks are awarded even if the final answer is wrong.

Question B1: Computers left on 8 marks

A computer room has 12 computers. Each uses 120 W while in use and 60 W while idle. They are used for 6 hours each school day but left idle for the other 18 hours instead of being switched off. There are 190 school days a year. Electricity costs €0.20 per kWh, and each kWh causes 0.25 kg of CO2.

  1. (a)

    Part a: Calculate the energy wasted per school day, in kWh. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B1 part a

    12 × 60 W × 18 h = 12 960 Wh = 12.96 kWh.

    Marking: 1 mark for the method; 1 mark for converting to kWh.

  2. (b)

    Part b: Calculate the energy wasted per school year. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B1 part b

    12.96 × 190 = 2462 kWh (2462.4 kWh).

    Marking: Allow error carried forward.

  3. (c)

    Part c: How much money is wasted per year? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B1 part c

    2462.4 × €0.20 = €492 (€492.48).

    Marking: Allow error carried forward.

  4. (d)

    Part d: How much CO2 is caused by the wasted energy each year? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B1 part d

    2462.4 × 0.25 = 616 kg (615.6 kg) of CO2.

    Marking: Allow error carried forward.

Question B2: From food waste to compost 8 marks

A school canteen throws away 15 kg of food waste every school day (190 days a year). In a landfill, each kg of food waste releases 0.05 kg of methane. Methane traps 28 times as much heat as the same mass of CO2 over 100 years. Composting reduces the mass of the waste by 60%.

  1. (a)

    Part a: Calculate the total food waste per year. 1 mark

    Worked solution for question B2 part a

    15 × 190 = 2850 kg.

    Marking: 1 mark.

  2. (b)

    Part b: What mass of compost would be produced? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B2 part b

    The mass falls by 60%, so 40% remains: 2850 × 0.40 = 1140 kg.

    Marking: 1 mark for using 40%; 1 mark for the answer.

  3. (c)

    Part c: Calculate the CO2-equivalent emissions avoided each year by composting instead of using a landfill. Ignore any emissions from the compost heap. 3 marks

    Worked solution for question B2 part c

    Methane = 2850 × 0.05 = 142.5 kg. CO2-equivalent = 142.5 × 28 = 3990 kg ≈ 4.0 t.

    Marking: 1 mark for methane; 1 mark for multiplying by 28; 1 mark for the answer.

  4. (d)

    Part d: Explain why a well-managed compost heap releases much less methane than a landfill. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B2 part d

    Methane is made by microbes that live without oxygen (anaerobic decomposition), as in buried landfill waste. A compost heap that is turned regularly contains oxygen, so microbes break the waste down aerobically into CO2 and water instead.

    Marking: 1 mark for anaerobic conditions in landfill; 1 mark for oxygen/aerobic in the compost heap.

Question B3: Cycling to school 7 marks

Ana lives 4.0 km from school. Each school day she is driven to school and back home (8.0 km in total). The car emits 120 g of CO2 per km. There are 190 school days a year.

  1. (a)

    Part a: Calculate the CO2 emitted by these journeys in a year, in kg. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B3 part a

    8.0 × 190 = 1520 km. 1520 × 0.120 kg = 182.4 kg.

    Marking: 1 mark for distance; 1 mark for the answer in kg.

  2. (b)

    Part b: 30 students with the same journey start cycling instead. How much CO2 do they save in total? 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B3 part b

    30 × 182.4 = 5472 kg ≈ 5.5 t.

    Marking: Allow error carried forward.

  3. (c)

    Part c: The car averages 30 km/h and Ana cycles at 15 km/h. How many extra minutes per day does cycling take? 3 marks

    Worked solution for question B3 part c

    Car: 8.0/30 h = 16 min. Bike: 8.0/15 h = 32 min. Extra time = 16 minutes per day.

    Marking: 1 mark for each time; 1 mark for the difference.

Question B4: Reading climate data 7 marks

Average CO2 concentration measured at Mauna Loa, Hawaii: 1960, 317 ppm; 1980, 339 ppm; 2000, 370 ppm; 2020, 414 ppm.

  1. (a)

    Part a: Calculate the average rate of increase, in ppm per year, for each 20-year period. 3 marks

    Worked solution for question B4 part a

    1960–1980: 22/20 = 1.10; 1980–2000: 31/20 = 1.55; 2000–2020: 44/20 = 2.20 ppm per year.

    Marking: 1 mark each.

  2. (b)

    Part b: Describe what your answers show. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B4 part b

    The concentration is not just rising: the rate of increase is itself increasing (it doubled over the 60 years). The rise is accelerating.

    Marking: 1 mark for increasing concentration; 1 mark for the increasing rate.

  3. (c)

    Part c: Predict the concentration in 2030 if the 2000–2020 rate continues. 2 marks

    Worked solution for question B4 part c

    414 + 10 × 2.20 = 436 ppm.

    Marking: Allow error carried forward.
